# Tiny Light-and-Growth Note — Scholarly Derivative

Status: demonstration derivative only. This file does not claim publication readiness, causal proof, or generalization beyond the described fixture.

## Research Object

A seven-day exploratory observation compared two trays of fast-germinating seeds maintained in otherwise similar indoor conditions. One tray received twelve hours of lamp exposure per day and the second received four hours.

## Neutral Scholarly Prose

At the end of the seven-day observation period, the seedlings in the twelve-hour lamp-exposure tray were visually taller than those in the four-hour tray. Within this limited fixture, the observation is consistent with an association between the longer daily lamp-exposure condition and greater observed seedling height.

The observation does not establish that increased light exposure universally causes plants to grow taller. The fixture contained only two trays in one room, did not report quantitative height measurements, and did not control cultivar variation, lamp spectrum, temperature gradients, watering error, or measurement error. No inference is made here about other plant species, light intensities, growth durations, or environmental conditions.

## Claim Standing Review

| Claim | Class | Standing | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| The twelve-hour tray had visibly taller seedlings after seven days. | observation | local, unreplicated observation | retained with scope |
| More light always causes plants to grow taller. | universal causal claim | unsupported by this fixture | refused as stated |
| The result may motivate a narrower light-exposure hypothesis under comparable conditions. | hypothesis | candidate only | allowed for future testing |
| The fixture omitted several controls and did not test generalization. | limitation / non-claim | directly supported by the fixture description | retained |

## What Would Be Needed for Stronger Standing

A stronger causal or generalizable claim would require, at minimum, replicated experimental units, randomized or otherwise justified assignment, quantitative growth measurements, defined plant material, controlled watering and temperature, characterized light intensity and spectrum, an appropriate statistical analysis plan, and replication across the conditions to which any generalization is intended to apply.
